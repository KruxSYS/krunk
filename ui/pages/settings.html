Who turns names into addresses. Custom: Krunk's own list, in order, over the engine's secure DNS-over-HTTPS client; Windows' resolver is never asked (a resolver with a name and no address is looked up once through it).

The first one answers; the next is asked after 2 seconds or an error. UDP, TCP, DNS over TLS, HTTPS (HTTP/2 and HTTP/3), QUIC, or an sdns:// stamp. DNSCrypt stamps are kept but skipped.

Request headers

Krunk's user agent

What sites see unless you choose another below.

Applies immediately to every tab; no restart needed. Some sites serve other pages to other browsers.

Printable ASCII, 512 characters at most. Empty means the default.

A comma list like en-GB, en, de; the engine adds the weights. Empty: your Windows language. Also sets navigator.language; applies to every tab at once.

On (like Chrome): sites learn the browser brand and platform from the Sec-CH-UA headers and navigator.userAgentData, and more when they ask for it. Off: no Sec-CH-UA header on any request, also when a site asks (Accept-CH), and no navigator.userAgentData in pages and their workers. Applies to every tab at once, private ones too.

What navigator.userAgentData and the Sec-CH-UA, -Mobile, -Platform headers say, plus the high-entropy hints sites may ask for (platform version, architecture, bitness, model, full version list). Off: the engine's own values (Chromium on Windows). Sent on https pages only, like Chrome.

Updates

Krunk updates

A minute after start and then once a day, Krunk downloads a small signed file from its update server to see whether a newer version exists. The request carries Krunk's version and nothing about you. Updates are only installed when you choose Update now.
